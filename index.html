Analysis and discussion

        In metal singing there are two main general types that form many smaller subdivisions. These two are known as screaming and growling. 

        Screaming: 

        When someone wants to sing really loud or high they do a technique known as belting, 
        which refers to when you tilt your larynx (or epiglottis) located inside of your throat backwards and applying pressure to the width of your vocal cords. 
        Screaming is very similar; however, you just apply pressure to the edge of your vocal cords instead (Unknown (2011)). 
        This style of singing is much more prevalent in hardcore, black metal and thrash metal with it being compared to the squawks of birds or the high pitched 
        nasal like voice of a witch (Stelzner, C. (no date)). Some of the most common types of screams are: 

        Fry Screams – A scream that uses deep breaths being pushed out from the diaphragm to force air into the vocal cords. 
        The noise created is usually wet-sounding and contains vocal fry creating that high pitch creaky voice of an elderly woman (Masterclass (2021)). 

        Inhale Screams – These screams require the singer to keep inhaling constantly rather than exhaling. 
        This creates a more guttural like sound by channeling the echoe of airflow through the throat. 
        They sound similar to a raspy ghost squeal (Masterclass (2021)). 

        Kvlt Scream – This scream is very powerful, creating a high-pitched buzzing type of scream that is usually heard in black metal music. 
        It sounds like a banshee cry with the shrillness to its sound (Masterclass (2021)).

        Growling: 

        Growling can be brought on by activating your false vocal cords allowing them to vibrate over the top of your real ones. 
        Your false vocal cords sit just on top of your vocal cords and are just like them in the sense that they are still two thick folds of mucous membrane 
        in your throat (imagine a thin piece of flesh tissue) (Unknown (2011).). 
        The only difference is they cannot close as well therefore don't get utilized very often. 
        To activate imagine do a really deep sigh, that slight tickle (or growl) noise that your throat makes is a growl. 
        Growls can be used much more for range with the positioning of your tongue greatly affecting the noise that is produced (Stelzner, C. (no date)). 
        It is much more prevalent in genres such as deathcore and death metal where its different techniques allow many different sounds to be made to 
        engage the listener. Below are some of the most common types: 

        Low Growl – Mainly only used in death metal, they create guttural sounds from the diaphragm and are much more difficult to use when singing lyrics. 
        They are much deeper in tone and can often be described as grunting the way the sound is made (Masterclass (2021)). 

        Mid-range Growl – While very similar to low growls, the mid-range is a lot larger in what sound you can make. 
        It projects much deeper breaths through the false vocal cords and allows the singer to properly enunciate his words without having to struggle. 
        This is probably one of the most common types of metal sounds there is (Masterclass (2021)). 

        High range Growl (Pig squeal) - The high range growl is a lot different to the others, it involves the singer using his breath, diaphragm, 
        vocal cords and tongue to create a high pitch squeal very similar to the sound a pig would make. You cannot enunciate any words with this technique 
        however it is commonly used before the music goes through the breakdown, a section in which instruments sound much heavier in nature (Masterclass (2021)). 

        Now that we’ve looked at the two main types of singing in metal music, we need to look at their long-lasting physical effects on an individual. 
        Multiple sources and research have stated that screaming can be done very healthily without damaging your vocal cords at all. 
        Singer Will Ramos of the deathcore band Lorna Shore has learnt how to scream, squeal and hiss like a teakettle all while not jeopardizing his vocals 
        (Friesen, S. (2024)). The singers have to produce their vocals using special technique with such safety to allow them to keep touring and doing show 
        after show (NewsRadio, K.S.L. (2024)).
